# About the Creator

PlantMate+ was built by Maria Lavigina - a junior software developer and designer based in Edinburgh.

---

## Background

Maria holds a BA in Design from Central Saint Martins, London. Before moving into technology she
worked as a designer and illustrator, most notably receiving a commission from the National Museum
of Scotland for their permanent fashion exhibition - five original illustrations, each two metres
tall. She introduced herself directly to the museum's CEO, presented her portfolio, and was
commissioned on the spot. The first sketch was accepted without revision.

She moved to Edinburgh and, recognising that the future of creative work was increasingly
intersecting with technology, made a deliberate decision to learn to build the things she had
previously only been able to design. That decision came with a particular weight: her mother had
been a software developer, and for years Maria had believed that coding required a kind of
intelligence she didn't have - that it was a world of numbers and logic that had no place for
someone who thought in images and colour.

Three years later, she is a junior software developer, currently in her third year at Edinburgh
Napier University, and building full-stack applications from scratch.

---

## PlantMate+ as a project

PlantMate+ is both a portfolio piece and a genuine learning exercise in professional-grade
full-stack development. The goal is not just to build something that works, but to build something
that reflects the standards and architecture of a senior-level project - proper auth, a real
database, a documented API, thoughtful UX - while being honest about where she is in that journey.

The design quality in PlantMate+ comes directly from the professional background. The colour
system is a named, intentional brand palette - not defaults. The typography uses three custom fonts
chosen for personality and hierarchy. The animations are storyboarded interactions, not effects
added for their own sake.

The spotlight hover effect on the hero image - a second illustration revealed only where the cursor
moves, masked by a CSS radial-gradient tracking mouse position in real time - is a direct
translation of exhibition thinking into code. A literal spotlight. Revealing something hidden in
plain sight.

> "The typography choices, colour system, and animation decisions especially read like someone who
> thinks visually first and codes second - which is a real strength in frontend work."
